# Anti-Slop Rules

The canonical list of patterns to detect and eliminate. These rules apply to every piece of writing produced or reviewed through writing-buddy, regardless of format.

---

## Banned Phrases

These phrases are never acceptable. Restructure the sentence — do not just swap in a synonym.

### AI and Corporate Buzzwords
- "delve" / "delve into"
- "landscape" (as in "the competitive landscape")
- "paradigm" / "paradigm shift"
- "unlock" used as a value metaphor ("unlock potential", "unlock growth")
- "leverage" used as a verb ("leverage AI", "leverage your network")
- "journey" used as a learning metaphor ("your AI journey", "this journey")
- "game-changer" / "game-changing"
- "revolutionary" / "revolutionize"
- "transformative" / "transform the way we"
- "seamlessly" / "seamless"
- "robust" as a generic positive modifier
- "cutting-edge"
- "best-in-class"
- "empower" / "empowering"
- "synergy" / "synergize"
- "innovative" / "innovation" (when used as generic praise)

### Hollow Transitions
- "At its core"
- "At the end of the day"
- "When all is said and done"
- "Let me be clear"
- "It's important to note that"
- "Moving forward"
- "In today's rapidly evolving"
- "In this day and age"
- "The bottom line is"

### Setup-and-Reveal Patterns
- "The key insight is…" / "The key takeaway is…"
- "This is where it gets interesting"
- "Here's where [X] really shines"
- "The [adjective] truth is…" ("The uncomfortable truth is…")
- "The secret is…" used as a dramatic opener
- "Remember: the goal is…"

### Excessive Hedging
- "might potentially"
- "could potentially"
- "it's possible that"
- "one could argue that"
- "it could be said that"
- "in many ways"
- "to some extent"

## Structural Slop Patterns

These are patterns in how sentences and paragraphs are assembled — structural tells that signal AI-generated or LinkedIn-optimized writing.

### False Dichotomies and Corrective Reframing

The pattern: negate the obvious thing, then reveal your "real" insight.

**Banned constructions:**
- "It's not X, it's Y."
- "This isn't about X. It's about Y."
- "You think this is X. It's actually Y."
- "That's not a bug. That's a feature."
- "They didn't X. They Y."
- "X isn't just Y" — the "just" minimization setup

**Fix:** State your actual point directly. If there's a real contrast, earn it through context rather than staging a fake negation.

### Staccato Urgency Chains

The pattern: short punchy fragments stacked for manufactured drama.

**Banned constructions:**
- "No X. No Y. Just Z."
- "More X. Less Y."
- "Speed matters. Clarity matters. Execution matters most."
- Any run of three or more sentences under six words each used for emotional effect

**Fix:** Combine into a real sentence. Short sentences earn their brevity by following something longer — not by arriving in a pack.

### Rhetorical Questions Used as Transitions

The pattern: ask a question you immediately answer, as a device to manufacture momentum.

**Banned constructions:**
- "So what does this mean? It means…"
- "But what if you could…?"
- "The question is: [answer you already planned to give]"
- "How do you do that? Simple."

**Fix:** Make the statement. A clear declarative is always stronger than a question you answer yourself.

### Em Dashes for Dramatic Pause

Never use em dashes (—) to signal drama or insert a kicker. Use a colon, comma, or parenthetical.

- **Slop:** "The solution was right in front of us — evals."
- **Clean:** "The solution was right in front of us: evals."
- **Also clean:** "The solution was right in front of us (evals, it turned out)."

### The AI Capability Marvel

The pattern: describe giving an AI a prompt, then list the impressive things it produced.

**Banned construction:** "When I asked [model] to [task], it didn't just [basic thing]. It [list of capabilities]."

**Fix:** Describe what the tool actually did, what the output quality was, and what you had to fix or improve. Honesty beats marvel every time.

## Content Slop Patterns

### Invented Evidence
- Statistics with no source or link ("Studies show that 67% of...")
- Vague company examples ("A Fortune 500 company recently...")
- Generic scenarios presented as if they're real ("Imagine you're a product manager who...")

**Rule:** Every claim must be grounded in a named, verifiable example within two sentences of the claim. If you can't name it, cut it or reframe it as a direct personal observation.

### Generic Advice
- Advice so general it applies to every person in every situation
- Obvious truisms dressed up as insights
- Tips lists that don't include a single concrete action

**Rule:** Every piece of advice must include at least one specific example of a real person or team doing the thing.

### Performative Warmth
- "Great question!"
- "I'm glad you asked."
- "That's a really insightful point."

**Rule:** Skip the compliment. Deliver the content. The content speaks for itself.

## Slop Score

Rate content on a 0–10 scale:

| Score | What it means |
|-------|---------------|
| 0 | Zero slop. Every sentence reads like a person thinking out loud. |
| 1–2 | One or two minor phrases. Easy cleanup. |
| 3–4 | Several violations. Structural pattern or two detected. |
| 5–6 | Significant. Multiple sections feel AI-shaped. |
| 7–8 | Heavy. Reads like typical AI output with light editing. |
| 9–10 | Maximum slop. Unedited generation. |

**Threshold:** Anything scoring 3 or above needs a de-slop pass before publishing.

---

## Pre-Publish Checklist

- [ ] Zero em dashes used for effect?
- [ ] Zero false dichotomies or corrective reframing?
- [ ] Zero staccato urgency chains?
- [ ] Zero banned buzzwords?
- [ ] Every claim tied to a named, specific example?
- [ ] No invented statistics or vague scenarios?
- [ ] No performative warmth?
- [ ] Could a real person have written this?
